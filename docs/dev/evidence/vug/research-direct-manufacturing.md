# Eliminating the CAD → CAM → post → controller chain: state of the art and research frontier

Research memo, 2026-11-01. Web research only; no code. Every claim carries a URL. Where a source is vendor marketing, a forum post, or a single blog, that is flagged.

## 0. One-paragraph answer

Nobody has eliminated the chain in production. What exists is (a) one standard that was designed to do it — STEP-NC, ISO 14649 / ISO 10303-238 — which is technically alive (edition 3, 2022; further edition in draft, 2026) but commercially stalled behind a chicken-and-egg problem between CAM vendors, controller builders, and post-processor owners; (b) a set of *monitoring* protocols (MTConnect, OPC UA 40501/40502) that are read-mostly by design and so cannot carry the loop closed on their own; (c) a wave of "automatic CAM" products (CloudNC CAM Assist, Toolpath, Fusion hole/whole-part recognition) that remove the *human* from CAM but keep CAM, the post and G-code; (d) a few genuinely vertical stacks that do go design → motion without a user-visible G-code step — OMAX IntelliMAX (waterjet), Glowforge (laser), Carbide Create → Carbide Motion (router), Bambu Studio → printer (FDM) — all of them single-vendor and closed; and (e) a clear architectural answer on the real-time boundary from Klipper and LinuxCNC: plan on the host, execute on a dumb, clock-synchronised MCU/FPGA, keep safety in hardware. The research frontier is feature-free / geometry-native process planning (PARC's configuration-space planners, SDF/implicit toolpaths), B-rep feature recognition with GNNs/transformers, LLM-assisted G-code with geometric verification loops, and the NIST/STEP Tools digital-thread demos (STEP AP242 + AP238 + MTConnect + QIF) that close the metrology loop into the model.

## 1. Standards and protocols

### 1.1 STEP-NC: ISO 14649 and ISO 10303-238 (AP238)

**What it is.** STEP-NC extends ISO 10303 (STEP) with the ISO 14649 machining model (features, "workingsteps", tools, tolerances) and PDM data, standardised as AP238, intended to replace ISO 6983 / RS-274 G-code with a product-model-linked process description. ISO 14649 is the ARM-style "application reference model"; AP238 is the integrated STEP-form (AIM) of the same model with GD&T and PDM added. [Wikipedia: STEP-NC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEP-NC)

**Editions and content.**
- AP238 ed.1 2007; ed.2 2020 (ISO 10303-238:2020) added machining process definitions, semantic tolerances, machine-tool kinematics, additive manufacturing and "machining twin" entities (`machining_twin_relationship`, `machining_usage_relationship`) and aligned with AP242; ed.3 September 2022 harmonised with AP242 ed.3 / SMRL v9 (annotation leader-line entities; same schema id as ed.2). [STEP Tools AP238e2 notes](https://downloads.steptools.com/docs/stp_aim/notes_ap238e2.html), [STEP Tools AP238e3 notes](https://www.steptools.com/docs/stp_aim/notes_ap238e3.html)
- A further edition reached draft stage in May 2026 (single source: DigitalCNC blog by the CEO of a machining-prediction vendor; unconfirmed elsewhere). [DigitalCNC "STEP-NC turns twenty"](https://digitalcnc.ai/step-nc-turns-twenty/)

**Timeline of demonstrations (all pre-commercial).** 1999 ESPRIT/IMS projects led by Siemens; 2005 OMAC AP238 test forum; 2006 Airbus 5-axis demo in Toulouse; 2007 AP238 published; 2008 closed-loop titanium impeller at CCAT Hartford (Boeing, DMG, KTH, Mitutoyo, NIST, Sandvik, Scania, Siemens, STEP Tools) — probe results (Faro arm / scanning probe) fed back into the STEP-NC file and toolpaths corrected; 2009–2012 FANUC-developed STEP-NC control, on-machine measurement, tool-wear and accuracy prediction; 2014 IMTS Okuma / Boeing / Sandvik / ISCAR titanium mould (15–30% cycle-time improvement claimed); 2018 IMTS "digital twin" with DMG MORI DMC 125FD and Hyundai WIA XF6300 on Siemens 840D, STEP + MTConnect + QIF, Renishaw/Mitutoyo probing merged into the twin. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEP-NC), [STEP Tools 2008 CCAT demo](https://steptools.com/stds/stepnc/2008_ccat/), [Modern Machine Shop on IMTS 2018](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/machining-demonstration-shows-the-digital-twin-concept-in-action)

**Why it stalled (consensus across sources).**
1. Chicken-and-egg: shops will not change workflows for a format their controllers cannot read; controller builders will not implement a format nobody sends. [DigitalCNC](https://digitalcnc.ai/step-nc-turns-twenty/)
2. Post-processors are where machine builders, integrators and CAM vendors keep their differentiating knowledge; a neutral file threatens that. [DigitalCNC](https://digitalcnc.ai/step-nc-turns-twenty/)
3. The same neutral file produces different cuts on different controllers because each applies its own kinematics, smoothing and look-ahead — so "neutral" does not mean "deterministic". [DigitalCNC](https://digitalcnc.ai/step-nc-turns-twenty/)
4. No single owner: the standard spans CAD, CAM, controller, tooling and metrology; no one budget pays for it. [DigitalCNC](https://digitalcnc.ai/step-nc-turns-twenty/)
5. Implementation burden far above G-code; files are hard to hand-edit; data-model deficiencies and proprietary macro languages (Fanuc Macro B, Siemens ShopMill, Heidenhain) raise switching costs. [Springer 2025 review (STEP-NC in AM)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-025-15290-8), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEP-NC)
6. IMTS 2018 post-mortem: CNC and CAM vendors "must improve support for interpreting STEP models" and the MTConnect/QIF vocabularies were "not yet fully formalized". [MMS](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/machining-demonstration-shows-the-digital-twin-concept-in-action)

**Who still uses / pushes it.** STEP Tools (NY) remains the reference implementation; aerospace persistent-identifier work led by Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman with Sandvik, Seco, Mastercam, Verisurf, Hexagon, Renishaw, PTC, Siemens, Mazak, Datanomix (2026); Sandvik Coromant's Dig4ReMan with GKN Aerospace / Chalmers using AP242 + AP238. Academic work continues (2025 "low-code modular CAD oriented to machining features for STEP-NC", Computer-Aided Design). [DigitalCNC](https://digitalcnc.ai/step-nc-turns-twenty/), [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S027861252500072X), [NSRP shipbuilding STEP-NC report](https://www.nsrp.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/Deliverable-Step_NC_Demonstration_Final_Report-Step_Tools.pdf)

**Adjacent "neutral machine model" effort that is actually being adopted: MDES.** ModuleWorks' Manufacturing Data Exchange Specification (open, 2023) describes machine tools (kinematics, geometry, controller) for exchange between CAM, simulation and builders; adopted by Autodesk (Fusion can output MDES), DN Solutions, BobCAD-CAM, CHIRON, Cimsource, SolidCAM, PTC, ISCAR (tooling data). This is the realistic path to a portable *machine* description, which STEP-NC never got. [ModuleWorks announcement](https://www.moduleworks.com/moduleworks-announces-early-adoption-of-manufacturing-data-exchange-specification-mdes-by-autodesk-and-dn-solutions/), [CIMdata on BobCAD](https://www.cimdata.com/en/industry-summary-articles/item/25690-bobcad-cam-commits-to-manufacturing-data-exchange-specification-mdes), [CIMdata on ISCAR](https://www.cimdata.com/en/industry-summary-articles/item/29810-iscar-to-adopt-mdes)

### 1.2 MTConnect

Read-only by design ("MTConnect cannot start, stop, or modify a machine"); an agent exposes `/probe`, `/current`, `/sample`, `/assets` over HTTP REST (XML/JSON), with WebSocket streaming and MQTT added in agent 2.x; input from SHDR adapters or MQTT; reference agent is Apache-2.0. Its value for direct manufacturing is as the *telemetry* leg of a closed loop (it was the "what happened" channel in the 2016–2018 NIST/STEP Tools demos), never the command leg. [MachineMetrics: MTConnect vs OPC UA](https://www.machinemetrics.com/blog/what-is-the-difference-between-mtconnect-and-opc-ua), [mtconnect/cppagent](https://github.com/mtconnect/cppagent), [NIST digital thread](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/digital-thread)

### 1.3 OPC UA for machine tools (umati, OPC 40501) and for CNC systems (OPC 40502)

- OPC 40501-1 "Machine Monitoring and Job Management" v1.02.0, published 2024-11-01; scope statement: make a machine tool easy to integrate into higher-level IT (MES/SCADA/ERP/analytics); server facets are Monitoring, Job Management, Production, Errors & Alerts, Prognoses, KPI. The scope text never mentions control or commanding. [OPC Foundation reference, scope](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/MachineTool/v102/docs/1), [v1.02 docs](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/MachineTool/v102/docs/)
- OPC 40502 "OPC UA for CNC systems" v1.0 (2017-07-05): `CncInterface` entry point, data access, alarms, model-change events, optional file transfer facet; no control methods found in the public spec listing. [OPC 40502](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-40502)
- umati (VDW) is a community/marketing umbrella endorsing 40501; figures on its site (110 machines, 28 software solutions, 70 companies, 10 countries) are undated. Competes with the MTConnect-OPC UA companion spec. [OPC Foundation on umati](https://opcfoundation.org/?p=5376), [VDW umati](https://vdw.de/en/technology-and-standardisation/umati-universal-machine-technology-interface), [MTConnect on UMATI](https://www.mtconnect.org/umati)
- OPC UA itself is read-write with access control, so a vendor *can* expose a write/command surface, but the machine-tool companion specs standardise monitoring and job *lists*, not motion.

### 1.4 Proprietary controller APIs (the real write channel today)

- **Fanuc FOCAS** (FOCAS1/2, Ethernet port 8193 or HSSB): read position/feed/spindle/load, read/write parameters (password-gated), download programs, drip-feed; requires licensed library (A02B-0207-K732/K737). [Cybus FOCAS docs](https://docs.cybus.io/documentation/industry-protocol-details/focas), [IMD knowledge base](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/fanuc-focas-library-ethernet-cnc-data-acquisition-setup)
- **Siemens Sinumerik ONE**: "digital-native" CNC whose twin (Create/Run MyVirtualMachine, Run MyVNCK — the real NC kernel in software) is part of the product; Create MyCC lets OEMs add compile cycles inside the NCK real-time area (OEM contract, export-controlled). Marketing claims of "Open API" integration with any CAD/CAM are unverified. [Siemens Sinumerik ONE](https://www.siemens.com/el-gr/products/sinumerik/one/), [Siemens CNC software portfolio PDF](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:2325b2d61fb77219f991cd38a0b8fa6aec3c1e79/cnc-softwareportfolio-en.pdf), [Siemens/JANUS NX machine-tool simulation](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/janus-engineering-nx-machine-tool-simulation-with-true-digital-twin/)
- **Okuma OSP-P300**: Windows-based open architecture, free API, app store (Sandvik, Blum, Renishaw apps). Vendor-authored sources. [Okuma white paper](https://www.okuma.com/white-paper/osp-p300-cnc-control-delivers-process-efficiencies-and-enables-user-innovation)

### 1.5 G-code dialects

G-code is "standard" only at the RS-274/NIST core. grbl is a subset of LinuxCNC's RS-274NGC and files generally run one way (grbl → LinuxCNC) but not the other; Marlin and Klipper are 3D-printer dialects where `G10` means retract (vs. offset-table write in LinuxCNC/Fanuc) and `G54–G59`/`G10 L20` work-offsets are absent or macro-emulated in Klipper; Smoothie ships both a "reprap" and a "grbl" mode because the two worlds conflict. [LinuxCNC forum](https://forum.linuxcnc.org/38-general-linuxcnc-questions/39564-expanding-g-code-library?start=10), [Marlin issue #27049](https://www.Github.com/MarlinFirmware/Marlin/issues/27049), [LightBurn forum on Klipper offsets](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/marlin-gcode-f0/92944), [Smoothie grbl_mode](https://smoothieware.org/grbl-mode)

- **grbl** (ATmega328P): constant-acceleration look-ahead planner, >30 kHz step rate, AMASS step smoothing, real-time single-character commands (feed hold, overrides, `?` status) outside the G-code stream; G-code subset G0–G4, G10 L2/L20, G28/30, G38.x probing, G54–59, G92, G93/94. [grbl wiki](https://github.com/gnea/grbl/wiki)
- **Marlin**: the base of most printer firmwares and of Prusa's Buddy firmware ("Marlin as 3D printing core driver", input shaper borrowed from Klipper, GPLv3). [Prusa-Firmware-Buddy](https://github.com/prusa3d/Prusa-Firmware-Buddy)

### 1.6 Klipper's host/MCU split (the cleanest existing answer to "where does real time live")

- Host (Python + C helpers, e.g. a Raspberry Pi) parses G-code, runs look-ahead and a trapezoid generator, then per-stepper C threads run an iterative solver that computes the time of *every* step from acceleration physics and machine kinematics (no Bresenham); `stepcompress` then lossy-compresses step times into `queue_step(interval, count, add)` commands. The MCU only executes a timer schedule; "timer functions always run with interrupts disabled" and must finish in microseconds. [Klipper Code_Overview](https://www.klipper3d.org/Code_Overview.html)
- Three clocks (host monotonic, "print time" synced to the main MCU, MCU ticks); 32-bit MCU clocks extended by the host; multi-MCU drift corrected in `clocksync.py`. Commands normally arrive "at least 100 ms before the time of its first step"; >100 µs MCU delays cause jitter, >500 µs retransmits, >100 ms watchdog reboot. [Klipper Code_Overview](https://www.klipper3d.org/Code_Overview.html)
- Results: "each stepper event is scheduled with a precision of 25 µs or better"; 175K steps/s on 8-bit AVR, up to ~7.4M steps/s on STM32H723; multi-MCU; input shaping and pressure advance implemented on the host. Why on host: Python makes new kinematics/features easy; timing is identical regardless of the low-level electronics. [Klipper Features](https://www.klipper3d.org/Features.html), [stepcompress discussion](https://klipper.discourse.group/t/improved-stepcompress-implementation/3203)
- Network/API: Moonraker exposes HTTP + JSON-RPC over websocket/Unix socket/MQTT, `printer.objects.query`, gcode scripts, file upload (HTTP only), API-key/JWT auth, OctoPrint-compat upload endpoints (`[octoprint_compat]`) used by PrusaSlicer/OrcaSlicer. [Moonraker API](https://moonraker.readthedocs.io/en/latest/external_api/introduction/), [Fluidd slicer uploads](https://docs.fluidd.xyz/features/slicer-uploads/)
- Caveat from the CNC side: Klipper is "not a buffered motion control system like GRBL… more like Mesa FPGA or Remora with LinuxCNC", and it was designed for printers first; CNC offsets and probing cycles are macro work. [LinuxCNC forum: Klipper vs Marlin vs LinuxCNC](https://www.forum.linuxcnc.org/additive-manufacturing/53145-klipper-vs-marlin-vs-linuxcnc)

### 1.7 LinuxCNC: HAL and real time

- Layers: EMCTASK (interpreter → "Canon" calls → NML messages), EMCMOT (real-time trajectory planner `tpmod`, homing, kinematics modules, runs at the servo period, regenerates joint positions every cycle), EMCIO (spindle/coolant/tool change, slow), HAL underneath. Modes free/teleop/coord. [LinuxCNC code notes](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/stable/html/code/code-notes.html)
- HAL: components with typed pins (bit/float/u32/s32) wired by signals; threads are "a list of functions that runs at specific intervals as part of a realtime task" — a base thread (tens of µs, software step generation) and a servo thread (~1 ms, PID/motion). Vertical abstraction (integrator describes the machine) and horizontal abstraction (mill/lathe/robot share planners). [LinuxCNC HAL intro](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/stable/html/hal/intro.html)
- Kernel: PREEMPT_RT is closest to mainline, has best driver support and is required for Mesa Ethernet cards but "has the worst latency of the available systems"; RTAI lowest latency, worse peripheral support. [LinuxCNC system requirements](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/stable/html/getting-started/system-requirements.html)
- Where step pulses come from matters more than kernel latency: Mesa FPGA cards generate steps in hardware ("latency does not limit the step generation rate but rather the following error"); 50–100 µs latency is fine for hardware stepgen, <30 µs needed for software stepgen; the Mesa DPLL brings sampling jitter "to the ns region even with 100s of µs of access jitter". EtherCAT is a separate LinuxCNC component with low jitter vs TCP/UDP. [LinuxCNC forum: Mesa and latency](https://forum.linuxcnc.org/18-computer/21442-is-latency-an-issue-when-using-a-mesa-card), [Mesa stepgen thread](https://forum.linuxcnc.org/27-driver-boards/27417-mesa-card-and-step-generation), [EtherCAT thread](https://forum.linuxcnc.org/ethercat/51853-does-ethercat-supported-in-linuxcnc)
- Anecdote on why real time must not live in the GUI process: a user's PC locked up and "the machine kept running and finished the part, 3 hours after the pc failed". [LinuxCNC forum](https://www.forum.linuxcnc.org/38-general-linuxcnc-questions/53045-linuxcnc-vs-klipper)

### 2.2 Slicers (additive) — the chain is already shorter here

- PrusaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio *are* the CAM and the post; the "controller" is Marlin/Klipper/Buddy; the network leg is PrusaLink (local), Prusa Connect (cloud, "legacy" when configured via Physical Printer), OctoPrint/Moonraker upload, or Bambu MQTT/FTPS. [Prusa help: sending G-code via network](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/sending-g-codes-to-printer-via-network-prusa-connect-prusalink-octoprint_196761), [Fluidd](https://docs.fluidd.xyz/features/slicer-uploads/)
- **Bambu closed loop**: micro-lidar probes nozzle height, calibrates flow dynamics and scans the first layer (scan runs after layer 1 completes); chamber-camera AI "spaghetti" detection (marketing: 86 % confidence) pauses the print. January 2025 "Authorization Control" firmware closed the LAN API to third-party slicers (OrcaSlicer broke), then "Developer Mode" re-opened MQTT/FTP on the user's responsibility — a warning about building on a closed vendor loop. [Bambu X1 page](https://bambulab.com/en-ca/x1), [Bambu forum on lidar](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/is-lidar-a-gimmick/578), [All3DP on Authorization Control](https://all3dp.com/4/bambu-lab-limits-third-party-printer-control-with-new-security-update/), [3DPI](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/bambu-lab-controversy-deepens-firmware-update-sparks-backlash-240588/)
- **Prusa**: no native AI failure detection; third parties (Obico/SimplyPrint) bridge the Buddy camera through OctoPrint. [Prusa forum Obico guide](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/user-mods/octoprint-obico-ai-failure-detection-for-core-one-complete-setup-guide/), [SimplyPrint compat](https://simplyprint.io/compatibility/prusa-core-one-l-plus)
- **"Slicer inside the CAD" trend is a hand-off, not an integration**: Shapr3D 26.120 (July 2026) opens the active design in PrusaSlicer/EasyPrint; slicing happens in Prusa's software. Fusion's "3D Print utility" launches Bambu Studio/PrusaSlicer/Cura with an STL. ### 2.3 Research: feature recognition and geometry-native planning

- **B-rep feature recognition with deep learning** has moved from voxels/point clouds to graphs/transformers on the B-rep itself: BRepGAT (99.1 % face labelling), BrepMFR (2024, transformer GNN + domain adaptation for limited labels), BRepFormer (2025), BRepMAE (self-supervised masked autoencoder, 2026), Hierarchical CADNet. Graph methods handle intersecting features better; all still assume a feature taxonomy. [BRepGAT](https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/10/6/2384/7453688), [BrepMFR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167839624000529), [BRepFormer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07378), [BRepMAE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22701), [Nature Sci Rep 2021 on CAD-integrated MFR](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-01313-3)
- **Feature-free process planning** (Behandish, Nelaturi et al., PARC): configuration-space/morphological reasoning plus AI search plans milling and turning from geometry alone, "with or without knowledge of feature semantics"; the authors argue feature taxonomies "limit usability". This is the strongest research argument that a design app can plan manufacturing *from the solid* rather than from recognised features. [arXiv 1905.09434](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09434)
- **Implicit / SDF / voxel toolpaths**: a signed-distance field "encodes all of the information needed to instantly offset an object", so contour/offset/infill generation is trivial and nTop recommends writing slices/toolpaths directly rather than STL; gradient-informed implicit slicing (OpenVCAD, 2025) improves dimensional accuracy by keeping continuity along the build axis; SDF-based toolpath generators report up to 16.5 % shorter paths vs rectilinear infill; voxel CAM gives robust collision/material-removal checks at a large memory cost. [nTop B-rep vs implicit](https://www.ntop.com/blog/understanding-the-basics-of-b-reps-and-implicits/), [arXiv 2505.08093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08093), [Karakoç JMMP 2024](https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/163440654/jmmp-08-00199.pdf), [Hilbig et al. 2023](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/files/29599420/qwad027.pdf), [SAE 2018 voxel CNC](https://saemobilus-test.cld.sae.org/papers/multi-axis-voxel-based-cnc-machining-centrifugal-compressor-assemblies-f-0074-2018-12798)
- **LLM G-code**: GPT-4 "fails to output complete code that precisely models the specified shape" for anything beyond drilling; GLLM (2025) fine-tunes StarCoder-3B with RAG and verifies toolpaths against intent with Hausdorff distance in a self-correction loop. Lesson: generate into a verified geometric intermediate, never raw G-code. [arXiv 2307.14377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14377), [GLLM arXiv 2501.17584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17584), [Šket et al. APEM 2025](https://www.apem-journal.org/Archives/2025/APEM20-2_224-238.pdf), [KG + LLM process planning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13026)
- **Machining-aware generative design**: Fusion's Generative Design has a 2.5-axis milling constraint that filters every iteration so outcomes are stacked 2D profiles with chosen tool directions ("guaranteed 2.5-axis manufacturable", per Autodesk patents). This is DFM *inside* the design loop rather than after it. [Autodesk help: manufacturing methods](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-GenerativeDesign/files/GD-MFG-METHODS.htm), [US 11409262](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11409262)
- **DFM feedback in CAD today** is mostly third-party add-ins (Xometry for Fusion/SolidWorks/Onshape gives live DFM + price while modelling). [Xometry CAD add-ins](https://www.xometry.com/cad-add-ins)
- CAPP surveys worth reading for the taxonomy of what has been tried: Cranfield "smart ACAPP" survey (2018), Deja & Siemiatkowski (2013), Isnaini & Shirase (2014). [Cranfield](https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/handle/1826/14499?show=full), [Deja & Siemiatkowski](https://pub.pg.edu.pl/pl/download/118183-feature-based-generation-of-machining-process-plans-for-optimised-parts-manufacture), [IJAT review](https://www.fujipress.jp/?p=12256)

## 3. Closed-loop and direct-drive concepts

### 3.1 Direct machining from CAD (the literal "no G-code" lineage)

- **BYU DMAC (Direct Machining And Control)**, patented US 7,283,888: the controller reads the CAD/CAM geometry (NURBS/motion entities) straight into its motion buffer, "eliminates all intermediate files (APT, M&G code, STEP-NC, etc.)", keeps associativity with the CAD model; commercialised by Direct Controls (Provo, UT) for robotic milling with Catia/GibbsCAM/Unigraphics, and extended to CMM inspection via PC-DMIS. [US 7283888](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7283888), [MMS "Direct-from-CAM machining"](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/direct-from-cam-machining-streamlines-robotic-milling), [Machine Design](https://www.machinedesign.com/archive/article/21824199/direct-machining-from-cad), [BYU thesis (RMAC)](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/235)
- **G-code-free STEP-compliant lathe** (ASME IMECE 2004) and the NIST survey of STEP-compliant NC research. [ASME IMECE2004](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/IMECE/proceedings-abstract/IMECE2004/47047/75/299242), [NIST STEP-compliant NC](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=823523)
- **Vertical commercial stacks that already go design → motion** (all single-vendor): OMAX IntelliMAX LAYOUT (CAD) → MAKE (applies "advanced internal cutting models and built-in compensations for speed, acceleration, and backlash" and converts the toolpath into motion; "No G or M code" per a resale listing); Glowforge cloud "transforming the design into a motion plan, recognizing materials… determining safe operating parameters"; Hypertherm EDGE Connect/Phoenix with built-in cut charts that set plasma parameters automatically; LightBurn talks directly to Ruida/Trocen/TopWisdom/grbl lasers. [OMAX software](https://omax.com/software), [OMAX automation guide](https://www.omax.com/en/media-center/tips/automation-using-intellimax/), [Glowforge/Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/customers/glowforge), [Hypertherm EDGE Connect](https://www.hypertherm.com/hypertherm/edge/edge-connect/), [LightBurn about](https://lightBurnSoftware.com/pages/about-us)
- **Pick-and-place**: OpenPnP is the open reference — a Java host with vision (bottom vision, fiducials, visual homing) drives any motion controller through configurable G-code templates and regex reply parsing (grbl/TinyG/Marlin/Smoothie), with sub-drivers for feeders. It is a design-app-owns-the-machine architecture in miniature: the controller is a dumb motion executor; all intelligence (vision, placement correction) is in the host. [OpenPnP GcodeDriver](https://github.com/openpnp/openpnp/wiki/GcodeDriver)

### 3.2 Metrology feedback into the model (digital thread)

- NIST/DMDII pattern (2016–2018): STEP AP242 for "why" (tolerances, probe points), MTConnect for "what" (machine data), QIF for "how" (results), everything linked by UUIDs; later NIST work aligns geometry via AP242 shape aspects and execution data via time intervals. [NIST digital thread](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/digital-thread), [Hardwick slides](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/04/18/hardwick_technology_for_the_digital_thread.pdf), [NIST GCR 20-024](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/gcr/2020/NIST.GCR.20-024.pdf)
- IMTS 2018: probing results merged into the twin and colour-coded against nominal; a QIF file validated by Mitutoyo CMM; one shop could resume another's job "without the program data or inspection criteria regressing"; feed-override changes visible in the twin. Compensation back into the toolpath was shown in 2008, not 2018. [MMS](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/machining-demonstration-shows-the-digital-twin-concept-in-action), [STEP Tools 2008](https://steptools.com/stds/stepnc/2008_ccat/)
- STEP-NC closed-loop literature: ISO 14649 Part 16 inspection model "enables a closed process chain"; G-code "is an obstacle" to it; open STEP-NC controllers with on-machine measurement; a review of STEP-NC-compliant CNCs and closed-loop possibilities. [Robotics & CIM 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0736584506001141), [Procedia CIRP case study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827115011130), [open STEP-NC controller](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283173295_Architecture_and_implementation_of_closed-loop_machining_system_based_on_open_STEP-NC_controller), [review 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327758674_A_review_of_STEP-NC_compliant_CNC_systems_and_possibilities_of_closed_loop_manufacturing)
- What ships: Renishaw closes the loop to *offsets* (Equator gauging → machine offsets; Renishaw Central auto-updates machine variables), not to the CAD model; "connecting production data back into design" is Siemens/Renishaw's stated direction. [Renishaw Equator process control](https://www.renishaw.com/en/45468.aspx), [Siemens NX blog on Renishaw](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/nx-manufacturing/high-precision-low-scrap-manufacturing-renishaws-digital-advantage-with-nx-cam/), [USPTO in-process digital twinning](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12498706)
- Framework standard: ISO 23247 (digital twin framework for manufacturing; Part 1 2021-10-21; Part 5 "digital thread" 2026) and NIST AMS 400-2 use cases. [ISO 23247-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/87425.html?browse=tc), [NIST use cases](https://www.nist.gov/publications/use-case-scenarios-digital-twin-implementation-based-iso-23247)

### 3.3 Machine twins in the CAM

Fusion Machine Builder: a machine definition (kinematics tree, axis limits, pivot offsets) linked to a post that "must have machine simulation capabilities" because "toolpath information is passed through the post before being displayed"; collision pairs are configured per kinematic node. Autodesk's own caveat: simulation "does not replace checking the NC code". Siemens goes further by running the real NC kernel (VNCK) inside NX. [Fusion Machine Builder](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/learn-how-machine-builder-works-in-autodesk-fusion/), [collision checks](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-CAM/files/MFG-MACHINE-MANAGE-COLLISION-CHECKS.htm), [Siemens VNCK](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/janus-engineering-nx-machine-tool-simulation-with-true-digital-twin/)

### 3.4 Zoo / KittyCAD

Zoo's Design API (KittyCAD brand) is a geometry engine over REST; the public `machine-api` repo (Rust, 449 commits, no license shown) is a "standardized local interface to any machines used for manufacturing physical things": `GET /machines`, `POST /print` with STL + `machine_id`, backends USB/serial, Moonraker, Noop, a `bambulabs` directory; it embeds a slicer config (PrusaSlicer INI) per machine. Zoo's hosted "Factory" API is read-only job listing with materials/finishes. It is the closest thing to a CAD-vendor-owned machine API, and it is thin. [KittyCAD/machine-api](https://github.com/KittyCAD/machine-api), [Zoo Factory API](https://zoo.dev/docs/developer-tools/api/factory), [Zoo Design API](https://zoo.dev/design-api)

## 4. What a design app must own to drive a machine directly

| Concern | Evidence for where it lives today | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinematics model** | Fusion Machine Builder, MDES, STEP-NC ed.2 kinematics, LinuxCNC `kins` modules, Klipper per-kinematics solvers | The app needs a declarative machine description (MDES is the one with adoption) plus forward/inverse kinematics it can execute, not just display. |
| **Tool library** | ISO 13399 cutting-tool data (used at IMTS 2018), MDES tooling (ISCAR), CloudNC/Toolpath "use the shop's library" | Tool geometry + holder + assembly is a first-class input to both toolpath and collision; vendors publishing ISO 13399/MDES removes the manual step. |
| **Stock / fixture model** | STEP-NC files model "product, process, fixtures, and cutting tools"; Toolpath proposes workholding; Boeing's four stage models in the 2018 part | Stock and fixtures are design objects with their own revision history; in-process stock (voxel/SDF) is the natural representation. |
| **Collision** | Fusion per-node collision pairs; voxel CAM "simplified collision checking"; Siemens runs the real kernel | Must be checked on the *post-kinematics* motion, which is why Fusion routes through the post; a direct-drive app checks on its own joint-space plan. |
| **Feed/speed physics** | Adaptive clearing (engagement-based), OMAX cutting models, Hypertherm cut charts, STEP-NC feed/speed optimisation from tolerance + sensor data | Process physics is per-process and per-material; the app owns the model, the machine reports the result (load, override). |
| **Safety interlocks** | E-stop as hardwired physical input into a safety relay/PLC → STO; software STO/safety sub-functions exist but sit on a certified hardware path (ISO 13849, IEC 61508) | Never in the design app, never in the host OS; the host may *request* stop, hardware *enforces* it. [Control Design](https://www.controldesign.com/articles/2021/standards-and-options-for-e-stops/), [Rockwell AT207](https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/at/safety-at207_-en-p.pdf), [ABB STO](https://library.e.abb.com/public/98442c13507f53dcc1257a090041d949/PDC6%20EN%20REVB%202011%20STO.pdf), [Siemens Safety Integrated](https://cache.industry.siemens.com/dl/files/069/109478069/att_854492/v1/SINUMERIK_Safety_Integrated_en_en-US.pdf) |
| **Real-time motion** | Klipper: host plans every step, MCU executes a timestamped schedule ≥100 ms ahead; LinuxCNC: servo thread 1 ms in PREEMPT_RT, step pulses in Mesa FPGA or EtherCAT drives; grbl: everything in one 8-bit MCU with a 30 kHz ISR | The boundary should sit at a *timestamped command queue* between a non-real-time planner and a hard-real-time executor (MCU/FPGA/drive). The OS need only be soft-real-time if the queue depth covers its jitter; the controller (if any) becomes the executor, not the interpreter. |

Where the boundary should sit, concretely: (1) design app / planner — non-real-time, any OS, owns geometry, kinematics, tools, stock, collision, physics, and emits a joint-space trajectory as timestamped segments; (2) executor — dedicated MCU, FPGA or servo drive with its own clock, synchronised to the host (Klipper's clocksync), executing steps/commands at the requested time with microsecond precision and failing safe on queue underrun (Klipper watchdog, LinuxCNC following-error); (3) safety — hardwired E-stop/STO/limits in a safety relay or PLC outside both. A PREEMPT_RT kernel on the host buys a shorter queue (lower latency to react to probes/overrides), not correctness.

## 5. (a) Ten design principles for "direct manufacturing baked into a CAD app" (ranked)

1. **Plan on the host, execute on a clock-synchronised dumb executor.** Compute every step time from physics and kinematics in the app and ship a timestamped queue to an MCU/FPGA that does nothing but fire at the right tick. [Klipper Code_Overview](https://www.klipper3d.org/Code_Overview.html)
2. **Keep safety out of software you wrote.** E-stop, STO and limits are hardwired through a safety relay/PLC; the app can only request a stop. [Control Design on e-stops](https://www.controldesign.com/articles/2021/standards-and-options-for-e-stops/)
3. **Ship geometry and intent, not machine words; verify geometrically before anything moves.** STEP-NC's lesson is that process intent linked to the product model is right, and LLM/G-code work shows unverified command text is wrong; check toolpaths against the model (e.g., Hausdorff) before release. [STEP-NC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEP-NC), [GLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17584)
4. **Make the machine a declarative model the app owns (kinematics, limits, tools, fixtures, stock), in an adopted format.** MDES is the machine description with real CAM/builder adoption; ISO 13399 for tools. [ModuleWorks MDES](https://www.moduleworks.com/moduleworks-announces-early-adoption-of-manufacturing-data-exchange-specification-mdes-by-autodesk-and-dn-solutions/)
5. **Plan from the solid, not from recognised features.** Feature-free configuration-space planning handles interacting features that taxonomies cannot; use feature recognition as a hint, not a gate. [Behandish & Nelaturi, arXiv 1905.09434](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09434)
6. **Use an implicit/SDF in-process model for stock, offsets and collision.** A distance field makes offsetting, slicing and gouge/collision checks cheap and representation-agnostic across mill, printer and laser. [nTop on implicits](https://www.ntop.com/blog/understanding-the-basics-of-b-reps-and-implicits/)
7. **Constrain the design by the process while designing, not after.** Fusion's 2.5-axis generative constraint shows manufacturability can be guaranteed by construction. [Autodesk GD manufacturing methods](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-GenerativeDesign/files/GD-MFG-METHODS.htm)
8. **Close the loop into the model with typed metrology, keyed by persistent IDs.** STEP for why, MTConnect for what, QIF for how, all tied by UUIDs so probe results land on the feature that produced them. [NIST digital thread](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/digital-thread)
9. **Treat the controller as a replaceable executor; do not depend on a vendor's closed loop.** Bambu's January 2025 lock-out of third-party slicers shows what happens when the loop is owned by the printer vendor. [All3DP](https://all3dp.com/4/bambu-lab-limits-third-party-printer-control-with-new-security-update/)
10. **Hardware-abstract the machine with typed pins and fixed-period threads so mills, lathes, printers and robots share planners.** LinuxCNC's HAL "vertical and horizontal abstraction" is the proven pattern for one planner, many machines. [LinuxCNC HAL intro](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/stable/html/hal/intro.html)

## 5. (b) Protocols a new system should speak first

| Priority | Protocol | Role | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Klipper MCU protocol + Moonraker JSON-RPC** | Executor command queue (steps, heaters, endstops) and host-side control/status | Open, documented, clock-synced, multi-MCU, proven at millions of steps/s; gives printers, routers and lasers on cheap boards immediately. [Klipper](https://www.klipper3d.org/Code_Overview.html), [Moonraker](https://moonraker.readthedocs.io/en/latest/external_api/introduction/) |
| 2 | **grbl v1.1 streaming + real-time characters** | Talk to the installed base of hobby/pro-sumer CNC, laser and pick-and-place controllers | Largest installed base; subset-of-RS-274 so files also run on LinuxCNC; real-time overrides/hold out of band. [grbl wiki](https://github.com/gnea/grbl/wiki) |
| 3 | **LinuxCNC HAL / NML (or EtherCAT via its component) with Mesa hardware stepgen** | Industrial servo machines, probing, hardware step generation | Hard-real-time path with FPGA stepgen and following-error semantics; HAL is the abstraction layer. [LinuxCNC HAL](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/stable/html/hal/intro.html) |
| 4 | **MTConnect (client)** | Ingest status/axis/load/override streams from any industrial CNC | Read-only, HTTP/JSON, Apache-2.0 agent; the standard telemetry leg of the digital thread. [cppagent](https://github.com/mtconnect/cppagent) |
| 5 | **OPC UA with OPC 40501 / 40502 (client)** | Job status, alarms, tool data on umati-labelled machines; vendor write surfaces where exposed | Only standardised route to European/Japanese builders' data; read-write framework even if companion specs are monitoring-centric. [OPC 40501-1](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/MachineTool/v102/docs/1) |
| 6 | **STEP AP242 + AP238 (export) and QIF (import)** | Carry design, tolerances, process intent out; bring inspection results back | The only neutral, ISO-backed way to make feedback land on the model; STEP-NC export is cheap once intent is in the app. [STEP Tools AP238e2](https://downloads.steptools.com/docs/stp_aim/notes_ap238e2.html), [NIST digital thread](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/digital-thread) |
| 7 | **MDES + ISO 13399 (import)** | Machine and tool descriptions from builders and tool vendors | Adopted by Autodesk, DN Solutions, SolidCAM, PTC, ISCAR, BobCAD; replaces hand-built machine definitions. [CIMdata ISCAR/MDES](https://www.cimdata.com/en/industry-summary-articles/item/29810-iscar-to-adopt-mdes) |
| 8 | **Fanuc FOCAS (later, licensed)** | Program load, drip-feed, offsets on the largest industrial controller base | Only practical write channel into Fanuc; licensed and gated, so defer. [Cybus FOCAS](https://docs.cybus.io/documentation/industry-protocol-details/focas) |

Not worth speaking first: Bambu MQTT/FTPS (vendor can and did close it), Siemens Create MyCC (OEM contract, export-controlled), Prusa Connect cloud (Prusa itself calls the slicer path "legacy"; PrusaLink/Moonraker-style local HTTP is the durable leg).

## 6. Gaps and caveats

- No authoritative 2024–2026 survey of STEP-NC adoption exists; the "draft edition May 2026" and the 2026 Lockheed/Sandvik items come from one vendor blog.
- umati adoption figures are undated marketing; OPC 40501 and 40502 scope text does not state an exclusion of control, it simply does not include it.
- OMAX's "no G/M code" is from a resale listing, not OMAX; the IntelliMAX product page is content-free.
- Bambu's AI internals are not documented; the 86 % figure is marketing.
- Zoo's machine-api shows no license or last-activity date on the fetched page.
- The DigitalCNC STEP-NC article is written by a vendor of machining-prediction software and frames the gaps accordingly.
